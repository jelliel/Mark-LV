# Fact-check

## Decompose first

A claim usually contains several checkable parts. "The council banned the book
in 553 and it stayed banned until 1965" has four: the council, the ban, the
year, and the end date. Check them separately — a claim that is half right is
not "mostly true", it is a claim with a wrong part, and the wrong part is the
finding.

## Sources, in this order

1. The document itself: the decree, the statute, the paper, the transcript, the
   dataset. Quote the sentence that decides it.
2. The issuing institution's own record.
3. Contemporaneous reporting.
4. Later scholarship, which is where misreadings propagate.

A quotation found only on quote-aggregator sites is unverified. Find the work
and the passage, or mark it `(attribution unverified)`.

## Dates and editions

Check the edition, the translation and the date of the source you actually
read. A claim sourced to a 1911 encyclopaedia about a 2024 policy is not wrong
because it is old — it is wrong because it is about a different world.

Also check retractions and corrections: a paper that was withdrawn still appears
in search results, and a number that was corrected still circulates.

## Verdicts

Use one of these, and nothing softer:

- **Supported** — a primary source states it, in these words or equivalent.
- **Partly supported** — some elements hold, and the report names which fail.
- **Contradicted** — a primary source states the opposite.
- **Unsupported** — no adequate source found, after a real search.
- **Unverifiable** — the claim cannot be checked from available material.

## Report the strongest version

Check the claim as its most careful proponent would state it, not the weakest
version that is easy to knock down. Then check whether the strong version
survives.
